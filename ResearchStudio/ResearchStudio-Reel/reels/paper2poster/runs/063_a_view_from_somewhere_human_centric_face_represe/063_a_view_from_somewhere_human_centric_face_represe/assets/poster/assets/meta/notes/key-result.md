# Key Result

Core claim: AVFS models predict face similarity more accurately than semantically trained baselines on both same-stimuli and novel-stimuli triplets, while better reflecting human judgment uncertainty (higher Spearman's r on odd-one-out entropy).

Supporting detail: Annotator-specific masks generalize to triplets of entirely novel faces, indicating the embedding space is more closely aligned with the human mental representation of faces than label-trained spaces.

Narration: Across both the same-stimuli and the novel-stimuli evaluations, the AVFS models predict human similarity judgments more accurately than baselines trained on semantic labels, and they better capture the uncertainty in human judgments as measured by Spearman correlation. Importantly, the annotator-specific masks generalize even to triplets made entirely of novel, previously unseen faces, which shows the embedding space is far more closely aligned with the human mental representation of faces than spaces learned from rigid categorical labels.
