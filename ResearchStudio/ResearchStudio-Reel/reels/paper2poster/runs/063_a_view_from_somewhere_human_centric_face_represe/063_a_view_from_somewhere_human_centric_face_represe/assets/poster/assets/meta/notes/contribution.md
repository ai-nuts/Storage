# Contribution

Core claim: The authors release AVFS, a dataset of 638,180 human triplet judgments over 4,921 faces annotated with annotator identity and demographics, and introduce a conditional framework that learns a continuous, human-interpretable face embedding space.

Supporting detail: Annotator-specific masks let the model study how each person weighs embedding dimensions, revealing that annotators are not interchangeable and that sociocultural background shapes similarity decisions.

Narration: Their answer is A View From Somewhere, or AVFS. They collect a dataset of six hundred thirty-eight thousand triplet similarity judgments over nearly five thousand faces, and crucially, each judgment records the identity and self-reported demographics of the annotator who made it. On top of this data they build a conditional learning framework that produces a continuous, low-dimensional, and human-interpretable embedding space, along with per-annotator masks that reveal how different people weigh different visual dimensions.
