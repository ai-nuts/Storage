# Motivation

Core claim: Face similarity is inherently continuous and observer-dependent, so a representation aligned with human perception can avoid problematic semantic labels while still supporting diversity analysis.

Supporting detail: Prior work either cannot embed faces outside the training set or pools all judgments, ignoring the stochasticity within and between individual annotators.

Narration: The authors argue that face similarity is fundamentally continuous and depends on who is doing the judging. If we could learn a representation directly aligned with human perception, we could sidestep problematic semantic labels entirely while still measuring diversity. Existing psychological-embedding methods fall short: they either cannot embed new faces beyond the fixed training set, or they pool every judgment together, throwing away the meaningful differences within and between individual annotators.
