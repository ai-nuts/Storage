# Dataset / Benchmark

Core claim: SiT comprises 60 scenes totaling roughly 60K images and 12K point cloud frames, with about 470K 2D annotations and 320K 3D annotations; each clip is 20 seconds of 10 Hz sequential data yielding 9 seconds of trajectory. It defines four benchmarks: 3D pedestrian detection, 3D tracking, trajectory prediction, and end-to-end motion prediction.

Supporting detail: Detection uses distance-based AP at 0.25/0.5/1.0/2.0 m; tracking uses sAMOTA/AMOTA/AMOTP/MOTA/MOTP/IDS; prediction uses ADE and FDE over K modes with a 2-second input and 7-second future horizon; annotated classes extend beyond pedestrians to car, bus, truck, cyclist, and motorcyclist. Indoor and outdoor scenes in downtown Seoul.

Narration: In total, SiT contains 60 scenes, which amounts to about sixty thousand images and twelve thousand point cloud frames, carrying roughly four hundred and seventy thousand two-dimensional annotations and three hundred and twenty thousand three-dimensional annotations. Each clip is twenty seconds of sequential data sampled at ten hertz, producing nine seconds of trajectory represented as pose vectors. On top of this data, the paper defines four benchmarks. Three-D pedestrian detection is scored with average precision based on center distance at thresholds of a quarter, a half, one, and two meters. Three-D tracking uses standard multi-object tracking metrics. Trajectory prediction takes two seconds of past motion and predicts seven seconds ahead, scored with average and final displacement error over the best of K candidate trajectories. And an end-to-end task runs from raw sensors all the way to future bounding boxes and trajectories.
