# Ablation Study

Core claim: Removing the bidirectional curriculum (IBC w/o Bidirectional) consistently degrades performance, and additionally removing the auxiliary agent (IBC w/o Bidirectional & Auxiliary, i.e., naive reset-free RL) degrades it further, especially in object-manipulation environments, confirming both components are necessary.

Supporting detail: The effect varies by task: bidirectional curriculum helps little in the simple Tabletop Manipulation state space, and the auxiliary agent is less effective in Point-U-Maze where the initial state is far from the goals. The auxiliary agent's episode ratio drops toward zero as the forward agent converges (Figure 6), confirming the implicit curriculum vanishes on schedule.

Narration: The ablation study peels the method apart to show that both pieces earn their keep. First, removing the bidirectional curriculum causes a consistent drop in performance, demonstrating the value of gradually guiding the agent from easy initial states and goals to harder ones. Then, further removing the auxiliary agent, which reduces the method to naive reset-free reinforcement learning, causes an additional and larger drop, particularly in the object-manipulation tasks. The gains are task-dependent: the bidirectional curriculum matters little in the simple Tabletop Manipulation environment, and the auxiliary agent is less helpful in Point-U-Maze, where the start is already far from the goal. The authors also confirm the implicit curriculum behaves as intended: the fraction of episodes the auxiliary agent intervenes in falls toward zero once the forward agent is fully trained.
