# Takeaway

Core claim: Highlighting the tokens a person is likely to edit, rather than the tokens the model found improbable, makes programmers faster, more surgical in their edits, and is strongly preferred, generation probabilities alone are not enough.

Supporting detail: Because products like Copilot already track edits to AI generations as a performance metric, that telemetry could train an open-world edit model to bring these benefits to real-world code assistance.

Narration: The lesson is simple but pointed. What you highlight matters more than whether you highlight, and the right thing to highlight is where people are likely to edit, not where the model happened to be unsure. Generation probabilities alone are not enough. Encouragingly, tools like Copilot already log the edits people make to AI suggestions, so that same signal could train an open-world edit model and carry these speed, precision, and preference gains into everyday coding.
