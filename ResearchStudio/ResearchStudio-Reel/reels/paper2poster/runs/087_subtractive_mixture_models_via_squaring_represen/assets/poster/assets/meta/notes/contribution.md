# Contribution

Core claim: The paper introduces a general framework to represent non-monotonic mixtures via squaring inside tensorized probabilistic circuits (NPC²s), shows they subsume square-root-of-density, PSD, and Born-machine models, and proves an exponential expressiveness advantage over monotonic circuits.

Supporting detail: It derives a simple tractable squaring algorithm over layers and provides broad empirical evidence on continuous, discrete, UCI, and language-model-distillation tasks.

Narration: This work makes four contributions. First, a general framework for representing subtractive mixtures by squaring, expressed in the language of tensorized probabilistic circuits. Second, it shows these squared non-monotonic circuits generalize several apparently different models with negative parameters, including square-root-of-density models, positive semi-definite kernel models, and Born machines from quantum physics. Third, it proves an exponential lower bound: functions a single squared circuit encodes compactly need exponentially more units in any monotonic circuit. And fourth, it backs this with experiments on real-world data.
