# Method

Core claim: Kernel PCA is cast as inf g(W) − f(ΓW) over a Stiefel manifold, then dualized so the finite-dimensional dual variable H replaces the infinite-dimensional directions; the indicator of the Stiefel manifold yields a nuclear-norm term π(H) = Tr√(H⊤GH) whose gradient is computed via a small s×s SVD.

Supporting detail: The standard KPCA dual is solved with L-BFGS; robust/sparse variants add a Moreau-envelope term Ψ⋆(H) and are optimized with a difference-of-convex (DCA) algorithm, using closed-form proximal operators for the Huber and ε-insensitive losses. Only s×s SVDs are ever needed, never the n×n one.

Narration: The method starts by writing Kernel PCA as the minimization of a difference of two convex functions over the Stiefel manifold of orthonormal frames. A general dualization proposition then converts this primal problem, whose variables live in a possibly infinite-dimensional feature space, into a dual problem whose variable H is a finite n-by-s matrix, perfectly suited to gradient descent. The indicator function of the Stiefel manifold turns, in the dual, into a nuclear-norm term the authors call pi of H, equal to the trace of the square root of H-transpose G H. Its gradient only requires the singular value decomposition of a small s-by-s matrix, not the full n-by-n Gram matrix, which is the source of the speedup. For the standard problem they run L-BFGS. To promote robustness or sparsity, they add a Moreau-envelope term to the objective and optimize with a difference-of-convex algorithm, plugging in closed-form proximal operators for the Huber and epsilon-insensitive losses.
