# Takeaway

Core claim: Curating high-locatability street views and injecting human reasoning knowledge from geo-games lets a two-stage fine-tuned LVLM both beat prior geo-localization models and explain its predictions, using a fraction of the training data.

Supporting detail: Data quality plus human-derived reasoning can substitute for sheer data scale, pointing toward interpretable, resource-efficient geo-localization.

Narration: The takeaway is simple but powerful. You do not need millions of street-view images to build a state-of-the-art geo-localization model. You need the right images and the right knowledge. By quantifying which street views are actually locatable and by borrowing the reasoning strategies humans use to win geo-games, GeoReasoner matches or beats specialist models with a fraction of the data, and, crucially, it explains every prediction. It is a case study in how data quality and human inference knowledge can stand in for brute-force scale, opening a path to geo-localization that is both interpretable and resource-efficient.
