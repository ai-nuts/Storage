# Problem

Core claim: Automated design of graph-structured molecules that optimize target properties is hard: chemical space is discrete and enormous, and compressing connected molecular objects into faithful, learnable feature representations is nontrivial.

Supporting detail: Manual drug discovery is slow and expensive, motivating AI that can efficiently and effectively navigate a vast, physically constrained molecular domain.

Narration: Designing molecules that optimize a property, like binding tightly to a viral protein, is a central goal in drug discovery, yet hard to automate. The chemical space is discrete and astronomically large, making exploration difficult, and compressing a molecule's atom-and-bond graph into a faithful, learnable representation is itself nontrivial.
