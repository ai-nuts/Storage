# Method

Core claim: A single-head, one-layer Transformer with softmax self-attention and a two-layer ReLU MLP is trained by minimizing hinge loss on prompts drawn from a subset of binary classification tasks. Each input carries in-domain-relevant (IDR) patterns that decide the label plus irrelevant patterns; the analysis tracks the gradient-descent training trajectory of the attention and MLP weights.

Supporting detail: Generalization is evaluated two ways: in-domain (test query and task from the training distribution) and out-of-domain (test query from a shifted distribution whose relevant patterns are linear combinations of the training patterns). Key quantities are α, the fraction of context examples sharing the query's IDR pattern, and β, the norm of relevant patterns.

Narration: The model is deliberately minimal but nonlinear: one self-attention head with a softmax, followed by a two-layer perceptron with ReLU activation. Training augments each query with a length-l context of example pairs, embeds them into a prompt, and minimizes a hinge loss over prompts sampled from a small subset of binary classification tasks. Every input contains relevant patterns that determine its label and irrelevant patterns that do not. The authors follow the gradient-descent dynamics of the query, key, value, and MLP weights, and evaluate the resulting model both in-domain and under a distribution shift where the test relevant patterns are linear combinations of the training ones. Two quantities drive the guarantees: alpha, the fraction of context examples that share the query's relevant pattern, and beta, the magnitude of the relevant features.
