# Method

Core claim: Robust training minimises a mean-squared loss plus a coefficient λ times a penalty on the network's input-output Jacobian at each training point. The authors translate the joint convergence of the MLP and its Jacobian into a tensor program and apply the Master theorem to prove GP convergence, then define the finite JNTK from inner products of the parameter-gradients of the output and of the Jacobian components. Showing the JNTK stays constant in the wide limit linearises the training ODE, whose t→∞ solution is a kernel regressor fntk built from the limiting JNTK Θ rescaled by a diagonal matrix Λ.

Supporting detail: The rescaled kernel is Θλ = Λ Θ Λ with Λ₀₀ = 1 and Λᵢᵢ = √λ; bounding parameter movement uses the 1-, 2-, and ∞-norms (the standard NTK proof needs only the 2-norm) because of the Jacobian regulariser.

Narration: Here is how it works. Robust training minimises the usual mean-squared error plus lambda times a penalty on the network's Jacobian at each training point. To handle the joint object, the network and its Jacobian together, the authors recast the convergence question as a tensor program and apply the Master theorem. They then define the finite JNTK from the parameter-gradients of both the output and each Jacobian component. The crucial step is showing this kernel does not move during training; that turns the learning dynamics into a linear differential equation, and its infinite-time solution is a kernel regression using the limiting JNTK, rescaled by a simple diagonal matrix that carries the regularisation strength lambda.
