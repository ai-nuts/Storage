# Contribution

Core claim: The paper surveys adaptive mechanisms in biology — metaplasticity, homeostasis, inhibition, and forgetting — and proposes concrete ways to translate them into the SO model and Hopfield networks via adaptive learning rate, enhanced modularity, and modified activation rules.

Supporting detail: It frames biological constraints not as opposites of flexibility but as a scaffold that lets degrees of freedom expand productively, and lays out a research agenda for algorithmically defining and testing these mechanisms.

Narration: This work makes two linked contributions. First, it distills key mechanisms of adaptation in biological systems, with a special focus on metaplasticity and homeostatic regulation, plus inhibition and forgetting. Second, it proposes concrete avenues to incorporate these features into the self-optimization model and Hopfield networks more broadly — by making the learning rate adaptive with respect to synaptic weights or the energy landscape, by enhancing modular structure, and by changing the activation rule. The aim is deep learning tools that recapitulate the advantages of biological systems and can plausibly model living and adaptive systems across levels of complexity.
