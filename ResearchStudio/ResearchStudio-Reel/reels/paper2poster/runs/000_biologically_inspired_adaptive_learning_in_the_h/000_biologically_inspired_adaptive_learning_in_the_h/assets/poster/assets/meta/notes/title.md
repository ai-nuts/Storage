# Title

How do living systems learn so much from so little, adapting with sparse data and minimal energy while artificial deep networks demand massive datasets and constant retraining? This work by Aisha Belhadi at the Okinawa Institute of Science and Technology looks to biology for answers. It distills three mechanisms of biological adaptation — metaplasticity, homeostasis, and inhibition — and proposes concrete ways to fold them into the Hopfield-network self-optimization model, aiming for deep learning tools that learn the way living systems do.
