# Method

Core claim: LightSB directly minimizes KL(π*‖π_θ) by parameterizing the adjusted Schrödinger potential v_θ as an unnormalized Gaussian mixture. This yields a tractable objective L(θ) plus closed-form conditional distributions π_θ(x₁|x₀) and normalization c_θ(x₀), optimized by plain stochastic gradient descent.

Supporting detail: Because the KL reduces to a difference of expectations of log c_θ and log v_θ, it admits Monte-Carlo estimation from samples; the learned plan reconstructs the full SB drift g_θ in closed form, so no process simulation is needed during training.

Narration: LightSB starts from the goal of minimizing the Kullback-Leibler divergence between the true entropic optimal transport plan and a parameterized plan. The obstacle is that we do not know the true plan. The key move is to exploit the known form of that plan and parameterize only the adjusted Schrödinger potential. The authors choose an unnormalized Gaussian mixture for this potential. Under this choice, two things become tractable in closed form: the conditional distributions of the plan, and the normalization constant. Proposition 3.1 rewrites the KL objective as a simple difference of two expectations, of the log normalization under the source and the log potential under the target, which can be estimated by Monte Carlo and optimized with ordinary stochastic gradient descent. No min-max, no iterative proportional fitting, and no simulation of trajectories during training. Once trained, the same potential gives a closed-form drift, so the full bridge process can be reconstructed directly.
