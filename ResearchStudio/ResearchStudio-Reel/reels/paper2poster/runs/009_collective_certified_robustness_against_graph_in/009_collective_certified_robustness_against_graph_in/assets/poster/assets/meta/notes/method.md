# Method

Core claim: Built on node-aware bi-smoothing (edge- and node-deletion randomized smoothing), the method exploits GNN locality: an injected node only harms a target if at least one message-passing path survives smoothing. It upper-bounds the message-interference probability p(E_v), derives a certifying condition, and models the worst-case attacker who maximizes non-robust target nodes under budget ρ (injected nodes) and τ (edges per injected node). This budget-allocation problem is a BQCLP, relaxed to an LP for tractability.

Supporting detail: Two relaxations are proposed: Collective-LP1 uses a standard quadratic-term linearization (O(ρ²|T|) extra variables); Collective-LP2 is a customized reformulation that combines the quadratic term first, needing only O(ρ|T|) extra variables, improving both solution quality and speed. The LP always has a larger feasible region than the BQCLP, so its certified ratio is a sound lower bound.

Narration: The method builds on randomized smoothing, specifically node-aware bi-smoothing, which randomly deletes edges and nodes to blur the attacker's influence. The crucial observation is locality: an injected node can only harm a target if at least one message-passing path between them survives the smoothing. The authors upper-bound the probability of that message interference, turn it into a certifying condition, and then model a worst-case attacker who tries to make as many target nodes non-robust as possible under a budget on injected nodes and edges. That worst-case problem is the binary integer quadratic program. Because it is NP-hard, they relax it to a linear program, and they offer two versions. The standard relaxation needs many extra variables, while their customized reformulation, Collective-LP-two, first collapses a quadratic term into a single vector, cutting the extra variables from order rho-squared to order rho and improving both quality and speed. Since the linear program has a larger feasible region than the integer one, its answer is always a sound lower bound on the true certified ratio.
