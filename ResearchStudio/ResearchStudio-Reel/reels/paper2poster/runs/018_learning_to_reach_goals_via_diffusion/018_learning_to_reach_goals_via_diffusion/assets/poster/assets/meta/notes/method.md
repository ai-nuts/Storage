# Method

Core claim: Merlin views an offline trajectory in reverse: starting from a goal state, a forward diffusion transformation pushes states away from the goal, and a goal-conditioned policy is trained to reverse these transitions, needing only a single denoising step per environment step at inference.

Supporting detail: A theorem shows that maximizing the log-likelihood of goal states under the reverse diffusion process reduces to behavior cloning with hindsight relabeling; the forward process is constructed either by a fixed rule, a parametric model (Merlin-P), or a non-parametric nearest-neighbor stitching in latent space (Merlin-NP).

Narration: Here is how Merlin works. Take a trajectory from the offline dataset and read it backwards, starting from its final goal state. Applying a forward diffusion transformation at each step produces states that drift progressively away from the goal, exactly like adding noise in an image diffusion model. Merlin then trains a goal-conditioned policy to reverse this drift, step by step, back toward the goal. The key theoretical result is that maximizing the likelihood of the goal states under this reverse process is equivalent to plain behavior cloning on the relabeled data, so no value function is ever needed. Because the state space itself is the diffusion space, the policy needs only one denoising iteration for each environment step, instead of the many iterations typical diffusion policies require. The authors offer three ways to build the goal-departing trajectories: a fixed construction, a learned parametric forward model in Merlin-P, and a non-parametric model in Merlin-NP that connects nearby states through nearest-neighbor stitching in a learned latent space.
