# Takeaway

Core claim: By learning detector noise with a score-based diffusion model and coupling it to a differentiable waveform, SLIC delivers unbiased gravitational-wave likelihoods without assuming Gaussian, stationary noise, a step toward scalable, artifact-robust inference.

Supporting detail: Current limits are the 4 s segment length and single-detector, MALA-based sampling; extending to longer segments, multi-detector coherent analysis, and advanced samplers like flowMC is the clear path to real high-dimensional GW events.

Narration: The takeaway: you no longer have to assume Gaussian, stationary noise. By learning the real noise distribution with a diffusion model and coupling it to a differentiable waveform, SLIC produces an unbiased likelihood straight from detector data. This proof of concept, currently single-detector and four-second segments, points toward fast, scalable, unbiased inference for future gravitational-wave observations.
