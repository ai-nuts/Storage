# Contribution

Core claim: The paper introduces context shift, a term for the semantically meaningful changes in the underlying data-generation process, and identifies three complementary methods for addressing it: human intuition and expert knowledge, dynamic benchmarking, and clarifying a model's limitations.

Supporting detail: It grounds the framing in three "model organism" computer-vision domains, facial expression recognition, deepfake detection, and medical diagnosis, showing how implicit benchmark assumptions cause real-world errors.

Narration: This is a perspective paper that reframes the robustness problem. Its central contribution is the concept of context shift, which describes the semantically meaningful upstream factors, sample selection bias, adversarial perturbation, and non-stationarity, that drive the distribution shifts we observe. On top of that framing, Groh identifies three practical methods for closing the benchmark-to-production gap, and illustrates all of them through three recurring case-study domains: facial expression recognition, deepfake detection, and medical diagnosis.
