# Headline Numbers

Core claim: - 48.6% to 94.8%: accuracy range of one FER model across 7 benchmarks (random baseline 14.2%) - 3-15%: state-of-the-art accuracy drop on faithfully re-created ImageNet and CIFAR-10 test sets - 2% and 8%: likelihood a leading DFDC detector gave to real deepfakes of two world leaders - 128,154 videos / 960 actors: size of the DFDC deepfake benchmark

Supporting detail: - 2.7%: share of the second-darkest Fitzpatrick skin type in one dermatology evaluation benchmark (1 person for the darkest)

Narration: A few numbers anchor the argument. A single facial expression model spans 48.6 to 94.8 percent accuracy across seven benchmarks, next to a 14.2 percent random baseline. Faithfully recreated ImageNet and CIFAR-10 test sets cut state-of-the-art accuracy by three to fifteen percent. A leading deepfake detector gave only two and eight percent likelihood to real deepfakes of world leaders. The DFDC benchmark holds 128,154 videos from 960 actors. And one dermatology benchmark included just 2.7 percent of the second-darkest skin type, and a single person of the darkest.
