# Takeaway

Core claim: Equilibrium propagation does not need symmetric weights: eliminating finite-nudge bias with a Cauchy integral and penalizing Jacobian asymmetry with a homeostatic loss lets asymmetric networks scale to ImageNet-level tasks with only a small gap to the ideal symmetric case.

Supporting detail: Because functional (Jacobian) symmetry is weaker than weight symmetry, this points to homeostatic processes as a biologically plausible route to training physical neural substrates.

Narration: The takeaway is that equilibrium propagation does not actually need perfectly symmetric weights. By removing finite-nudge bias exactly with a Cauchy integral, and by encouraging functional symmetry of the Jacobian with a homeostatic loss instead of the weights, asymmetric networks can finally scale to ImageNet-level tasks with only a small gap to the ideal case. Because functional symmetry is a weaker, more achievable condition than weight symmetry, this opens a biologically plausible path to training real physical neural substrates, and hints that brains might rely on similar homeostatic mechanisms.
