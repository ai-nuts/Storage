# Headline Numbers

Core claim: Front collisions 14 → 9 (~36% reduction) and distance-to-reference failures 35 → 28 (~20% reduction) versus the best baseline; the paper reports up to ~35% improvement on some metrics.

Supporting detail: Optimal upsampling factor w ≈ 50; 180 hours of training data with 60 hours each for validation and test; final policy trained for 40 epochs.

Narration: "To put concrete numbers on it: front collisions drop from fourteen to nine against the strongest baseline, roughly a thirty-six percent reduction. Distance-to-reference-trajectory failures fall from thirty-five to twenty-eight, about a twenty percent reduction. Side collisions go from fifty-five to forty-seven. Overall the paper reports improvements of around thirty-five percent on some metrics. The best upsampling factor is around fifty. The models were trained on one hundred and eighty hours of driving data, validated and tested on sixty hours each, with the final policy trained for forty epochs."
