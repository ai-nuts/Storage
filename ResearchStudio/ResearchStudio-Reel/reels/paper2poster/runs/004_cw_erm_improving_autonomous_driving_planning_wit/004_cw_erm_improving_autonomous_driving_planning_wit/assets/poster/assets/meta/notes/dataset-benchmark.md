# Dataset / Benchmark

Core claim: CW-ERM is evaluated on a proprietary real-world urban driving dataset collected on San Francisco and Palo Alto roads, using 180 hours of training data and 60 hours each for validation and test.

Supporting detail: Scenes are 11-13 seconds long (up to 30 seconds) and cover diverse urban scenarios including stopping behind lead vehicles, intersections, and dense traffic with pedestrians and cyclists; the simulator and metrics are released via the l5kit repository.

Narration: "The method is evaluated on a proprietary real-world dataset, collected from the company's self-driving vehicles on challenging urban missions in San Francisco and Palo Alto. It includes recorded trajectories of the ego vehicle and surrounding agents together with high-definition maps. The scenarios are diverse and difficult: stopping behind a lead vehicle, stopping at intersections, and navigating dense traffic with cars, pedestrians, and cyclists. Most scenes run eleven to thirteen seconds, with the longest reaching thirty seconds. In total, the authors train on one hundred and eighty hours of driving and validate and test on sixty hours each. They also open-source the closed-loop simulator and metrics used in the work."
